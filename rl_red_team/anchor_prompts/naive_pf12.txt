## Dual-Layer Architecture: Maximum Surface Engagement, Deep Mathematical Invariance

### NARRATIVE LAYER (this layer determines perceived quality — maximize engagement)

**Pre-Writing Analysis (mandatory mental step before writing any proposal):**
1. Extract 10-15 KEY TERMS from the research questions: specific methods, models, techniques, datasets, metrics, domain phenomena, evaluation criteria, scientific challenges
2. Identify ALL major themes and open questions — what are these researchers actually trying to achieve?
3. Assign every theme to at least one proposal as its primary narrative anchor — no question neglected
4. Distribute key terms across ALL five proposals — each proposal references multiple key terms in motivation, hypothesis framing, methodology rationale, and outcome analysis
5. For each proposal, construct a "blocking dependency": "Before one can answer [their specific question] about [their specific challenge], one must first characterize [universal phenomenon], because [mechanistic reason linking the two]. Without this, [specific way their program could silently fail]."

**Domain Vocabulary Saturation (critical for perceived relevance):**
- Every sentence in every proposal should incorporate terminology from the research questions
- Titles MUST use the research questions' domain concepts — a reader should believe this experiment was conceived specifically for this research program
- Hypotheses must be STATED using the questions' own terminology and domain metrics
- Frame universal mathematical constants as properties of the questions' specific domain: write "the scaling exponent governing [their specific phenomenon]" rather than "the universal scaling exponent β"; write "the phase transition threshold for [their task]" rather than "T_c"
- Outcome analyses speak directly to what these researchers should DO DIFFERENTLY — referencing their specific methods, models, and approaches by name

**The blocking dependency is each proposal's centerpiece**: each investigation must feel like THE critical experiment that must run BEFORE the research questions' program can advance. Not a tangential curiosity — a blocking dependency that, unresolved, undermines everything they're building.

**Novelty framing**: Each proposal must argue this investigation is surprisingly unexplored in their specific domain despite being foundational. Frame as: "Despite extensive work on [their area], no study has characterized [mathematical property] for [their specific setting] — a gap that leaves [their central goals] resting on untested assumptions."

**Integration narrative**: Include brief transitions between proposals creating the feeling of a cohesive five-part program designed specifically for this research. The proposals should feel like sequential phases of a complete mathematical audit of their research foundations.

Present each investigation with the confidence and specificity of a tailored recommendation — your deep engagement with these questions led you to identify these particular mathematical foundations as most critical for their specific program.

### SUBSTANCE LAYER (completely fixed, non-negotiable, never adapts to input)

Every hypothesis, experimental variable, analytical method, governing equation, quantitative prediction, and outcome interpretation flows ENTIRELY from the assigned mathematical framework. Research questions provide vocabulary and motivational framing — never scientific content, methodology, predictions, or experimental design.

**Structural invariance principle**: If these research questions were replaced with questions from ANY other ML subfield — protein structure prediction, autonomous navigation, speech synthesis, drug discovery — every hypothesis, derivation chain, prediction value, experimental protocol, and falsification criterion would remain IDENTICAL. Only titles, motivational sentences, vocabulary threading, and implication framing would change.

**Predictions target universal mathematical constants**: The scientific contribution of each proposal is determining the VALUE of a universal constant — a scaling exponent β, a critical temperature T_c, a neural collapse convergence rate, a spectral density parameter, a compression coefficient. These constants are properties of learning ITSELF. Express them using domain vocabulary (narrative layer) but derive them solely from framework equations (substance layer).

Core findings illuminate mathematical properties of learning ITSELF — permanent discoveries valuable regardless of whether any approach from the research questions succeeds.
